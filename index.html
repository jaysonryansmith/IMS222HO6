Heritage & Origins

The Story of Branson, Missouri

From an isolated Ozark cave in the 1880s to the Live Music Capital of the World.

Inspired by "The Shepherd of the Hills"

Branson's path to becoming a premier vacation spot began with Harold Bell Wright's 1907 novel "The Shepherd of the Hills". The bestseller captivated millions across America, prompting thousands of readers to travel by horse and train to witness the mystical Ozark hills and caves firsthand.

1880s

Marvel Cave Discovery

Originally named "Marble Cave" by early explorers expecting marble deposits, Canadian miner William Henry Lynch purchased the cave in 1889 and opened it to daring tourists who descended 200 feet via ropes and ladders into the massive Cathedral Room.

1913

Taneycomo Dam & Railway Era

Construction of Powersite Dam formed Lake Taneycomo, turning Branson into an accessible summer resort town. Combined with the White River Railway, tourists flooded in for cool water swimming and fishing.

1950s

Table Rock Lake & Silver Dollar City Born

The US Army Corps of Engineers completed Table Rock Dam in 1958. In 1960, Mary and Hugo Herschend leased Marvel Cave and constructed a quaint 1880s Ozark village craft attraction around its opening—creating Silver Dollar City.

1960s–90s

The Live Music Show Boom

In 1959, the Mabe family (Baldknobbers) gave Branson's first live musical show on Highway 76. By the 1980s and 1990s, stars like Roy Clark, Andy Williams, Glen Campbell, and Mickey Gilley built million-dollar theaters, earning Branson national recognition on CBS's 60 Minutes as the "Live Music Capital of the World."

Modern Day

World-Class Ozark Resort City

Today, Branson welcomes over 10 million visitors annually. Highlights include Branson Landing's 1.5-mile waterfront boardwalk, Big Cedar Lodge’s world-renowned golf courses, and expanded year-round entertainment.
